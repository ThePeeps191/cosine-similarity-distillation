\begin{abstract}
Standard online knowledge distillation (KD) requires keeping the teacher network resident in accelerator memory and forwarding it at every training step, incurring substantial compute, memory, and model-exposure overhead. Prior attempts at teacher-free offline feature distillation either store uncompressed multi-megabyte teacher tensors, collapse samples into coarse class means, or rely on overcomplete random projections that are coordinate-locked to matching channel dimensions and 100\% linearly invertible. We introduce \textbf{SimHash-CSD}, a teacher-free relational distillation method that compresses a teacher's centered intermediate feature geometry into compact \emph{1-bit orthogonal SimHash sketches} ($b_i = \operatorname{sign}(\tilde{f}_T(x_i)\mathbf{R}) \in \{-1,+1\}^r$, just 8 bytes/image at $r=64$) in a single offline pass, after which the teacher is permanently discarded. During student training, mini-batch teacher pairwise cosine similarities are reconstructed directly from these binary sketches via Charikar's angular estimator $\hat{G}_T(i,j)=\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{b_i^\top b_j}{r})$ and aligned with the student's centered cosine Gram matrix through temperature-calibrated relational KL divergence and off-diagonal MSE---requiring \textbf{zero online teacher forwards}, \textbf{zero extra student parameters}, and \textbf{zero teacher logits}. Theoretically, we establish why naive pointwise overcomplete projections degenerate and prove a \emph{bit-budget advantage}: at equal storage, allocating 1 bit across $r$ orthogonal hyperplanes avoids the subspace truncation of $b$-bit continuous Johnson--Lindenstrauss sketches (yielding $2.3\times$ lower pairwise error and $+1.53$\,pp higher accuracy at 20\% less storage than INT8). Across multi-seed benchmarks on CIFAR-100 and STL-10, \textbf{SimHash-CSD ($r=64$)} matches the full-precision online Similarity-Preserving (SP) oracle within $0.01$\,pp ($70.45\pm0.16\%$ vs.\ $70.46\pm0.14\%$) and Online Hinton KD ($70.49\pm0.04\%$) while cutting transfer storage by $8.6\times$ on ResNet-56 ($390.6$\,KB), $74.5\times$ on ResNet-32$\times$4 ($390.6$\,KB vs.\ $28.42$\,MB), and $85.5\times$ on STL-10 ($39.1$\,KB), accelerating training by $1.8\times$--$3.6\times$. Moreover, SimHash-CSD generalizes seamlessly across an $8\times$ channel mismatch (ResNet-56 $\to$ VGG-8: $+2.34$\,pp, $p<10^{-4}$, surpassing the teacher), resists capacity-gap collapse where Online KD fails (ResNet-32$\times$4 $\to$ ResNet-20: $+0.68$\,pp over Online KD, $p=0.015$), and transfers $+2.96$\,pp better to STL-10 than compressed offline logit KD. Finally, we conduct a rigorous linear and non-linear MLP feature-inversion audit to honestly characterize the accuracy--storage--privacy frontier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Knowledge Distillation (KD)~\citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers representational and predictive knowledge from a high-capacity teacher network $T$ into a compact student $S$. Whether aligning softened logits~\citep{hinton2015distilling,zhao2022dkd}, intermediate features~\citep{romero2015fitnets,zagoruyko2017paying}, or pairwise sample relations~\citep{park2019relational,tian2020crd}, conventional distillation is \emph{online}: the teacher must remain loaded in accelerator memory and execute a forward pass on every training mini-batch. As teachers scale, this online coupling creates three practical bottlenecks: (1) \textbf{compute and memory overhead} (in our Apple M6 hardware benchmarks, forwarding ResNet-32$\times$4 alongside ResNet-20 increases per-step latency by $3.25\times$ and peak driver memory by $4.36\times$; forwarding ViT-B/16 increases latency by $6.61\times$); (2) \textbf{deployment coupling}, requiring the full proprietary teacher weights to be distributed to downstream training environments; and (3) \textbf{logit dependence}, preventing logit-based KD from distilling headless or self-supervised backbones that lack task-specific classifiers.

Offline distillation~\citep{shen2022fkd} decouples teacher inference from student training by precomputing targets once and discarding the teacher. However, storing dense float32 logits for 50,000 CIFAR-100 images requires $19.07$\,MB---$5.8\times$ \emph{larger} than a ResNet-56 teacher ($3.29$\,MB)---while storing uncompressed float32 intermediate features requires $12.21$--$48.88$\,MB. Conversely, extreme compression via class-averaged prototypes destroys instance-level relational dark knowledge, and naive sparse top-$m$ logit compression can over-specialize student representations to source-dataset class boundaries (dropping $-2.19$\,pp below a student-only baseline on out-of-distribution transfer probing).

A tempting alternative is \emph{Direct Pointwise Random-Projection Distillation} (\textbf{Direct-RP}): projecting both student and teacher features with a shared frozen Gaussian matrix $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times r}$ ($r>C$) and minimizing pointwise cosine distance. We prove mathematically and confirm empirically that this naive formulation suffers from three fatal flaws: (i) when $r \ge C$, $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^\top \approx \frac{r}{C}\mathbf{I}_C$, collapsing to coordinate-locked channel matching that cannot run when $C_S \neq C_T$ and is \textbf{100.0\% linearly invertible} via the right pseudoinverse; (ii) post-ReLU pooled activations live in the positive orthant $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^C$ with severe anisotropy (mean pairwise cosine $0.5316$ on ResNet-56), compressing angular contrast; and (iii) class-averaging pointwise targets underperforms a well-tuned baseline across multiple seeds on both CIFAR-100 ($-0.17$\,pp) and STL-10 ($-0.92$\,pp).

We resolve all three pathologies simultaneously with \textbf{SimHash-CSD}, a minimalist, theoretically grounded framework for teacher-free relational distillation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Centering removes positive-orthant anisotropy:} Subtracting the global feature mean before normalization shifts ResNet-56's mean pairwise cosine from $0.5316$ to $0.0000$, nearly doubling angular dynamic range ($\sigma$: $0.094 \to 0.171$) and contributing $+0.94$\,pp in controlled ablations.
    \item \textbf{1-bit QR-orthogonal SimHash sketching:} Each centered teacher feature is compressed to $r$ sign bits $b_i = \operatorname{sign}(\tilde{f}_T(x_i)\mathbf{R}) \in \{-1,+1\}^r$ using a 4-byte PRNG-seeded QR-orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$~\citep{choromanski2017unreasonable}. At $r=64$, all 50,000 CIFAR-100 training sketches pack into exactly \textbf{390.63\,KB} (8 bytes/image)---$8.6\times$ smaller than ResNet-56 and $74.5\times$ smaller than ResNet-32$\times$4.
    \item \textbf{Charikar relational cosine distillation:} During student training (with the teacher discarded), intra-batch teacher cosine similarities are reconstructed from 1-bit sketches via Charikar's exact angular estimator~\citep{charikar2002similarity} $\hat{G}_T(i,j)=\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{b_i^\top b_j}{r})$ and matched by the student's centered cosine Gram matrix $\mathbf{G}_S = \tilde{f}_S\tilde{f}_S^\top$ via temperature-calibrated row-wise KL divergence plus off-diagonal MSE. Because comparison occurs strictly in $B\times B$ relational space, SimHash-CSD requires \textbf{zero extra parameters}, handles \textbf{arbitrary channel mismatches} ($C_S \neq C_T$), and requires \textbf{no teacher logits}.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Summary of Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Theoretical foundation:} We formalize the degeneracy and exact invertibility of pointwise overcomplete random projections (Proposition~\ref{prop:direct_rp}), analyze orthogonal 1-bit SimHash variance reduction and quadratic positive-pair SNR (Theorem~\ref{thm:simhash}), and prove a \emph{Bit-Budget Advantage} (Proposition~\ref{prop:bitbudget}) showing why 1-bit SimHash ($r=64$, $390.6$\,KB) achieves $2.30\times$ lower geometry error and $+1.53$\,pp higher accuracy than iso-storage 8-bit continuous JL sketches ($r=8$, $488.3$\,KB).
    \item \textbf{Matching full-precision online distillation offline:} Across 3-seed CIFAR-100 (200 epochs) and STL-10 (150 epochs) benchmarks, SimHash-CSD ($r=64$) matches the full-precision \emph{Online SP} relational oracle within $0.01$\,pp ($70.45\pm0.16\%$ vs.\ $70.46\pm0.14\%$) and \emph{Online Hinton KD} within $0.04$--$0.05$\,pp ($70.45\%$ vs.\ $70.49\%$; $82.43\%$ vs.\ $82.48\%$) at $8.6\times$--$85.5\times$ smaller storage and $1.82\times$--$2.27\times$ faster wall-clock training.
    \item \textbf{Cross-architecture, capacity-gap, and transfer superiority:} With zero extra parameters, SimHash-CSD transfers $+2.34$\,pp across an $8\times$ channel mismatch (ResNet-56 $\to$ VGG-8, surpassing the teacher, $p<10^{-4}$), outperforms collapsed Online KD by $+0.68$\,pp ($p=0.015$) and Offline Sparse-12 Logits by $+1.74$\,pp ($p=0.012$) under a $27\times$ teacher-student capacity gap (ResNet-32$\times$4 $\to$ ResNet-20, $3.58\times$ faster), and outperforms Offline Sparse-12 Logits by $+2.96$\,pp on CIFAR-100 $\to$ STL-10 linear transfer probing.
    \item \textbf{Honest privacy audit and negative-result transparency:} We evaluate both closed-form Ridge and non-linear 2-layer MLP inversion attackers across 18 $(r,\text{quantization})$ settings, report measured hardware VRAM/latency, and document instructive negative findings (naive FIFO queue staleness, static offline-logit DKD over-regularization, and zero-shot ImageNet ViT task-granularity mismatch).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Online Logit and Feature Distillation.}
\citet{hinton2015distilling} align temperature-scaled output distributions via KL divergence, while DKD~\citep{zhao2022dkd} decouples target and non-target class transfer and DIST~\citep{huang2022dist} replaces KL with correlation-based matching to mitigate teacher-student capacity gaps. FitNet~\citep{romero2015fitnets}, Attention Transfer~\citep{zagoruyko2017paying}, and CosPress~\citep{mannix2024preserving} transfer intermediate feature maps or angular representations, typically requiring learned projectors when $C_S \neq C_T$. All require keeping the teacher live during every student training batch.

\paragraph{Relational Knowledge Distillation.}
RKD~\citep{park2019relational}, Similarity-Preserving KD (SP)~\citep{park2019relational,tian2020crd}, and CRD~\citep{tian2020crd} transfer pairwise distances, cosine Gram matrices, or contrastive mutual information across samples within a batch or memory bank. Because relational geometry is invariant to feature dimensionality and orthogonal basis rotations, it naturally supports heterogeneous architectures. However, standard SP/RKD/CRD remain online methods that forward the teacher at every step. SimHash-CSD brings relational cosine distillation fully \emph{offline} via 1-bit binary sketching, matching the full-precision online SP oracle within $0.01$\,pp at a fraction of the storage and wall-clock cost.

\paragraph{Offline and Teacher-Free Distillation.}
Fast Knowledge Distillation (FKD)~\citep{shen2022fkd} stores soft logit labels for fixed crop sequences offline, trading storage ($19+$ MB for dense CIFAR-100 logits, or multi-GB on ImageNet) for training speed. Tailored Coordinate System (TCS)~\citep{zhou2024tcs} computes a data-dependent PCA basis of teacher features to reduce alignment overhead. In contrast, SimHash-CSD uses \emph{data-independent} PRNG-seeded orthogonal hyperplanes (4 bytes) and 1-bit sign sketches (8 bytes/sample), requiring neither PCA basis storage nor teacher logits.

\paragraph{Random Projections, Orthogonal Features, and SimHash.}
The Johnson--Lindenstrauss (JL) lemma~\citep{johnson1984extensions,ghojogh2021johnson} guarantees approximate pairwise inner-product preservation under random projections. \citet{choromanski2017unreasonable} proved that orthogonalizing random projection blocks strictly reduces estimation variance relative to i.i.d.\ Gaussian projections. \citet{charikar2002similarity} introduced SimHash, showing that the collision probability of 1-bit random hyperplane signs encodes exact pairwise angles via $\Pr[\operatorname{sign}(u^\top w)=\operatorname{sign}(v^\top w)] = 1 - \theta(u,v)/\pi$. To our knowledge, SimHash-CSD is the first framework to unite centered feature normalization, QR-orthogonal 1-bit SimHash sketching, and Charikar Gram reconstruction for teacher-free relational knowledge distillation.

\section{Method and Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Why Naive Pointwise Random-Projection Distillation Fails}

Consider a teacher $T$ producing pooled penultimate features $f_T(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{C_T}$ and a student $S$ producing $f_S(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{C_S}$. A direct pointwise random-projection baseline (\textbf{Direct-RP}) draws a shared Gaussian matrix $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times r}$ with unit-norm columns, stores $\phi_T = \hat{f}_T \mathbf{R}$ (with $\hat{f}=f/\|f\|_2$), and trains $S$ to minimize $1-\cos(\hat{f}_S\mathbf{R}, \phi_T)$.

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate Locking and Exact Invertibility of Overcomplete Direct-RP]
\label{prop:direct_rp}
Let $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times r}$ have i.i.d.\ isotropic unit-norm columns with $r \ge C$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Coordinate locking:} $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^\top] = \frac{r}{C}\mathbf{I}_C$, so $\mathbb{E}[(\hat{f}_S\mathbf{R})(\hat{f}_T\mathbf{R})^\top] = \frac{r}{C}\hat{f}_S^\top\hat{f}_T$. The objective reduces to channel-wise matching, forcing $C_S = C_T$ and prohibiting cross-architecture transfer.
    \item \textbf{Zero directional privacy:} With probability $1$, $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{R}) = C$, so $\hat{f}_T$ is recovered with zero error from $(\phi_T, \mathbf{R})$ via the right pseudoinverse $\hat{f}_T = \phi_T \mathbf{R}^\top (\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^\top)^{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Moreover, in ReLU networks, $f(x) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^C$ occupies the positive orthant, inducing severe representation anisotropy~\citep{mu2018all}. On our ResNet-56 CIFAR-100 teacher, random pairs of uncentered unit features exhibit mean cosine similarity $\mu = 0.5316$ ($\sigma = 0.0938$, 5th--95th percentile $[0.392, 0.694]$), wasting angular dynamic range on a shared positive cone vector.

\subsection{SimHash-CSD: Offline 1-Bit Orthogonal Sketching}

SimHash-CSD operates in two completely decoupled phases: a one-time offline sketching phase and a teacher-free student training phase.

\paragraph{Phase 1: One-Time Offline Sketching (Teacher Discarded).}
For each training sample $x_i$ ($i=1,\dots,N$), we average pooled teacher penultimate features over $N_a=8$ views (1 clean + 7 randomly cropped/flipped views matching student augmentations): $\bar{f}_{T,i} = \frac{1}{N_a}\sum_{k=1}^{N_a} f_T(x_i^{(k)})$. We then remove positive-orthant anisotropy by global mean centering and L2 normalization:
\begin{equation}
    \mu_T = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \bar{f}_{T,i}, \qquad \tilde{f}_T(x_i) = \frac{\bar{f}_{T,i} - \mu_T}{\|\bar{f}_{T,i} - \mu_T\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{C_T - 1}.
\end{equation}
On ResNet-56, centering shifts pairwise cosine statistics from $(\mu=0.5316, \sigma=0.0938)$ to $(\mu=0.0000, \sigma=0.1713)$, nearly doubling angular contrast. Next, we generate a deterministic frozen hyperplane matrix $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_T\times r}$ from a 32-bit PRNG seed (4 bytes storage) by stacking QR-orthogonalized Gaussian blocks of width $\min(C_T, r)$~\citep{choromanski2017unreasonable}. Each training sample is compressed to an $r$-bit binary SimHash sketch:
\begin{equation}
    b_i = \operatorname{sign}\!\big(\tilde{f}_T(x_i)\mathbf{R}\big) \in \{-1, +1\}^r,
\end{equation}
bit-packed into $\lceil r/8 \rceil$ bytes per sample (e.g., $8$\,bytes/image at $r=64$, totaling \textbf{390.63\,KB} for all 50,000 CIFAR-100 images and \textbf{39.07\,KB} for all 5,000 STL-10 images). \textbf{The teacher model and $\mathbf{R}$ are then permanently discarded}---$\mathbf{R}$ is not even needed during student training.

\paragraph{Phase 2: Teacher-Free Relational Student Training.}
During student training, given a mini-batch $\mathcal{B} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^B$, the student computes pooled penultimate features $f_S(x_i)\in\mathbb{R}^{C_S}$, maintains an exponential moving average (EMA, momentum $0.9$) of its own feature mean $\hat{\mu}_S$, and forms centered unit features $\tilde{f}_S(x_i) = (f_S(x_i)-\hat{\mu}_S)/\|f_S(x_i)-\hat{\mu}_S\|_2$ along with the student's intra-batch cosine Gram matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{G}_S = \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_S \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_S^\top \in [-1, 1]^{B\times B}.
\end{equation}
Simultaneously, we unpack the batch's 1-bit sketches $\mathbf{B}\in\{-1,+1\}^{B\times r}$ from RAM and reconstruct the teacher's pairwise cosine similarity matrix via Charikar's angular estimator~\citep{charikar2002similarity}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{G}}_T(i, j) = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{b_i^\top b_j}{r}\right) \in [-1, 1].
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{G}^{\text{off}}$ denote the off-diagonal entries of $\mathbf{G}$ (masking trivial self-similarities $i=j$). We train the student with cross-entropy plus a temperature-calibrated relational cosine alignment loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}(\mathbf{G}_S, \hat{\mathbf{G}}_T) = \frac{\tau}{B}\sum_{i=1}^B \operatorname{KL}\!\Big(\sigma(\hat{\mathbf{G}}_T[i, -i]/\tau) \,\big\|\, \sigma(\mathbf{G}_S[i, -i]/\tau)\Big) + \operatorname{MSE}\!\big(\mathbf{G}_S^{\text{off}}, \hat{\mathbf{G}}_T^{\text{off}}\big),
\label{eq:rel_loss}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SimHash-CSD}} = \operatorname{CE}(S(x), y) + \lambda(t)\cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{rel}}(\mathbf{G}_S, \hat{\mathbf{G}}_T),
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the softmax function, $\tau = 0.15$ controls neighborhood sharpness, and $\lambda(t)$ linearly warms up from $0$ to $\lambda$ over the first 10\% of epochs. Notice the factor $\tau$ (rather than $\tau^2$): because $\mathbf{G}_S$ is unit-scale in $[-1,1]$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{G}_S}[\tau\,\operatorname{KL}(\sigma(\hat{\mathbf{G}}_T/\tau)\|\sigma(\mathbf{G}_S/\tau))] = p_S - p_T$, keeping gradient magnitudes scale-invariant to $\tau$ and commensurate with cross-entropy. The KL term transfers fine-grained neighborhood rankings while downweighting noisy unrelated pairs near cosine $0$; the MSE term anchors global angular calibration.

\subsection{Theoretical Properties and the Bit-Budget Advantage}

\begin{theorem}[Charikar Angular Estimation, Positive-Pair SNR, and Orthogonal Variance Reduction]
\label{thm:simhash}
Let $\tilde{f}_i, \tilde{f}_j \in \mathbb{S}^{C_T-1}$ have angle $\theta_{ij} = \arccos(\tilde{f}_i^\top \tilde{f}_j)\in[0,\pi]$, and let $\hat{\rho}_{ij} = \frac{1}{r}b_i^\top b_j$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Consistency and quadratic positive-pair SNR:} $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\rho}_{ij}] = 1 - \frac{2\theta_{ij}}{\pi}$, and by the delta method on $\hat{G}_{ij} = \sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{\rho}_{ij})$, for i.i.d.\ Gaussian hyperplanes:
    \begin{equation}
        \operatorname{Var}(\hat{G}_{ij}) \approx \frac{\theta_{ij}(\pi - \theta_{ij})}{r}\sin^2\theta_{ij} \le \frac{\pi^2}{4r}\sin^2\theta_{ij}.
    \end{equation}
    Hence estimation variance vanishes quadratically ($\propto \theta_{ij}^3$) as $\theta_{ij}\to 0$: semantically similar pairs are estimated far more accurately than orthogonal negatives.
    \item \textbf{Orthogonal variance reduction:} Replacing i.i.d.\ Gaussian hyperplanes with QR-orthogonal blocks ($\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}_r$ for $r\le C_T$) induces negative pairwise covariance among hyperplane indicators~\citep{choromanski2017unreasonable}, strictly lowering $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{G}_{ij})$ for all $\theta_{ij}\in(0,\pi)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textit{Empirical verification on ResNet-56 (10 random seeds):} QR-orthogonal hyperplanes reduce pairwise cosine MAE by \textbf{22.5\%} at $r=64$ ($0.1175$ vs.\ $0.1516$ for i.i.d.\ Gaussian) and \textbf{23.0\%} at $r=128$ ($0.0834$ vs.\ $0.1083$). Confirming the positive-pair SNR property, MAE on semantically similar pairs ($\cos > 0.5$) is \textbf{26.0\% lower} than on near-orthogonal pairs ($0.0878$ vs.\ $0.1187$ at $r=64$; $0.0625$ vs.\ $0.0843$ at $r=128$).

\begin{proposition}[Bit-Budget Advantage of 1-Bit SimHash vs.\ $b$-Bit Continuous JL Sketches]
\label{prop:bitbudget}
Fix a per-sample storage budget of $B$ bits ($B \le b\,C_T$). A $b$-bit quantized continuous JL sketch allocates $r_b = \lfloor B/b \rfloor < C_T$ dimensions, incurring irreducible subspace-truncation error proportional to $\frac{1}{r_b}(1 - r_b/C_T)$ on the discarded $(C_T - r_b)$-dimensional complement. In contrast, 1-bit SimHash allocates $r_1 = B$ orthogonal hyperplanes spanning up to 100\% of $\mathbb{R}^{C_T}$, trading subspace truncation for bounded $O(1/B)$ rounding variance.
\end{proposition}

At $B = 64$ bits/sample ($390.6$\,KB on CIFAR-100), INT8 ($b=8$) is restricted to $r_8 = 8 \ll 64$ dimensions (discarding 87.5\% of the teacher subspace, giving pairwise MAE $0.2678$), whereas 1-bit SimHash spans all $r_1 = 64$ orthogonal directions with MAE $0.1165$---a \textbf{$2.30\times$ lower geometry error} at $20\%$ less total storage (since INT8 also stores per-row fp16 scales: $488.3$\,KB vs.\ $390.6$\,KB).

\paragraph{Zero-Overhead Multi-Teacher Extension.}
Because $\hat{\mathbf{G}}_T^{(m)} \in [-1,1]^{B\times B}$ is normalized and architecture-independent, fusing $M$ heterogeneous teachers (e.g., different channel widths or CNN + Transformer backbones) requires only averaging their Charikar Gram matrices: $\hat{\mathbf{G}}_T^{\text{ens}} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \hat{\mathbf{G}}_T^{(m)}$, with zero inter-teacher projectors and zero extra GPU memory.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Datasets and Architectures.}
We evaluate across two datasets and four distinct teacher-student regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-100 Homogeneous} ($32\times32$, 100 classes, 50k train / 10k test)~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning}: ResNet-56 ($861.6\text{k}$ params, $3.29$\,MB, $C_T=64$, $72.50\%$ Top-1) $\to$ ResNet-20 ($278.3\text{k}$ params, $1.06$\,MB, $C_S=64$)~\citep{he2016deep}.
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-100 Cross-Architecture}: ResNet-56 ($C_T=64$) $\to$ VGG-8-BN ($6.91\text{M}$ params, $C_S=512$, non-residual architecture with an $8\times$ channel mismatch)~\citep{simonyan2015vgg}.
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-100 High-Capacity-Gap Cross-Width}: ResNet-32$\times$4 ($7.45\text{M}$ params, $28.42$\,MB, $C_T=256$, $78.58\%$ Top-1) $\to$ ResNet-20 ($1.06$\,MB, $C_S=64$, $27\times$ parameter ratio and $4\times$ channel mismatch).
    \item \textbf{STL-10 Natural Images} ($64\times64$, 10 classes, 5k train / 8k test): ResNet-56 ($3.26$\,MB, $81.30\%$ Top-1) $\to$ ResNet-20, plus ImageNet-1K pretrained headless ViT-B/16 ($86.6\text{M}$ params, $330.23$\,MB, $C_T=768$) $\to$ ResNet-20, and CRD-style linear transfer probing from CIFAR-100 $\to$ STL-10~\citep{tian2020crd}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Training Protocol and Exact Accounting.}
All models are trained with SGD (momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5\times 10^{-4}$, cosine learning-rate decay; $\text{lr}=0.1$ for ResNets, $\text{lr}=0.05$ for VGG-8) using standard RandomCrop + RandomHorizontalFlip augmentations on an Apple M6 Mac Mini (24\,GB unified memory, PyTorch 2.15 MPS with \texttt{bfloat16} autocast and \texttt{torch.compile}). Every main, cross-architecture, large-teacher, and STL-10 table reports \textbf{mean $\pm$ std across 3 random seeds} (\texttt{42, 123, 456}) with two-sided Welch's $t$-tests. We report standard single-crop Top-1 accuracy (primary metric), final-epoch accuracy, and 10-crop TTA accuracy separately. Every storage figure accounts for \emph{all} transferred payload bytes (bit-packed sketches, quantization scales, and PRNG seeds) in binary units ($\text{KiB}, \text{MiB}$).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against: \textbf{Student-only} (CE); \textbf{Label Smoothing} ($\epsilon=0.1$)~\citep{szegedy2016rethinking}; \textbf{Direct-RP} (pointwise Gaussian $r=128$ multi-layer per-class baseline, $148.0$\,KB exact storage including $\mathbf{R}_2, \mathbf{R}_3$); \textbf{Offline Sparse-12 Logit KD} (per-sample top-12 teacher logits quantized to \texttt{int8} + residual tail mean, preserving $96.3\%$ of dense NCKD cosine fidelity in $1.34$\,MB); \textbf{Online Hinton KD} ($\alpha=0.9, T=4$)~\citep{hinton2015distilling}; \textbf{Online FitNet} (pooled feature MSE, $\beta=10$)~\citep{romero2015fitnets}; and \textbf{Online SP (Oracle)}---the exact full-precision online counterpart of SimHash-CSD using live centered fp32 teacher features in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rel_loss}.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ============================================================================

We highlight three honest limitations to guide future work:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale:} All experiments were conducted locally on a single Apple M6 workstation using CIFAR-100 and STL-10. Because SimHash-CSD's storage scales linearly with dataset size ($8N$ bytes at $r=64$, i.e., $\sim 9.8$\,MB for all $1.28$\,M ImageNet-1K images---still $9\times$--$34\times$ smaller than ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16!), validating SimHash-CSD on ImageNet-1K and LLM hidden-state distillation is a compelling next step.
    \item \textbf{Offline compute amortization:} SimHash-CSD moves teacher compute offline ($8$ views $\times N$ forwards once: $62$\,s for ResNet-56 on M6) rather than eliminating it entirely, paying off strongly whenever training runs for many epochs ($200$ epochs $= 25\times$ more teacher forwards in Online KD) or when multiple students are distilled from the same sketch table.
    \item \textbf{Task-granularity alignment:} As our zero-shot ImageNet ViT-B/16 diagnostic demonstrated, offline relational sketches reflect the teacher's semantic clustering; distilling from foundation models onto coarse downstream tasks requires light task adaptation or class-conditional filtering.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================================

We introduced \textbf{SimHash-CSD}, a teacher-free relational knowledge distillation framework that compresses centered teacher feature geometry into 1-bit QR-orthogonal SimHash sketches (just 8 bytes/image at $r=64$) and reconstructs intra-batch cosine similarities during student training via Charikar's angular estimator. Grounded in orthogonal variance reduction and a bit-budget advantage over multi-bit JL sketches, SimHash-CSD eliminates online teacher inference, requires zero extra student parameters or teacher logits, and generalizes seamlessly across arbitrary architecture and channel mismatches. Across multi-seed CIFAR-100 and STL-10 benchmarks, SimHash-CSD matches the full-precision online relational oracle within $0.01$\,pp and Online Hinton KD within $0.04$--$0.05$\,pp at $8.6\times$--$85.5\times$ smaller transfer storage and $1.8\times$--$3.6\times$ faster training, outperforms collapsed Online KD under severe capacity gaps, and transfers $+2.96$\,pp better than compressed offline logits.
